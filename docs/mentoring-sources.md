# Mentoring content and evidence

Reviewed September 7, 2026. Published in `_pages/recruitment.md` at `/openings/`, with mentoring at `#mentoring` and the lab's research workflow at `#research-with-ai`.

## Daniel's account

The four-stage progression, participation in existing lab projects, networking, conference presentations, talk practice, and support for professional recognition come from Daniel's description in this website update. The first year begins with an adviser-developed project suited to the student's skills; the second introduces a jointly designed follow-up; from year three, students increasingly lead their own research. Continued support is made concrete through dissertation, communication, and career development guidance.

The year labels describe a typical progression, not deadlines for independence, a guaranteed graduation timetable, or a promise of publication. Existing application instructions, recruitment availability, and funding wording are preserved.

## Five additions from mentoring guidance

| Addition | Supporting guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Agree on communication, responsibilities, feedback, and credit | [Cornell's Faculty Guide to Advising Research Degree Students](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/resources/faculty-guide-to-advising-research-degree-students/), sections on shared expectations and collaborative publishing. |
| Revisit research, skills, and career goals | [CU Boulder's mentoring resources](https://www.colorado.edu/graduateschool/services-resources/mentoring/student) connect advising plans, individual development plans, and annual evaluation. Cornell recommends at least annual review and career exploration beyond academia. |
| Invite feedback about the mentoring relationship | [National Academies, The Science of Effective Mentorship in STEMM (2019), Summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25568/chapter/2), recommendation 3, supports feedback and accountability. |
| Develop a network of mentors | The same National Academies synthesis, recommendation 5, supports multiple mentoring structures. CU Boulder also provides peer mentoring resources. |
| Adapt support and discuss sustainable working practices | Cornell's guide addresses individual circumstances, belonging, rest, and workload; the National Academies' recommendation 4 addresses identity and inclusive mentoring. |

These are practical commitments added at Daniel's request, not a claim that particular formal lab agreements or development plans already existed. The sources support the practices; they do not establish one universally best mentoring style or validate the lab's exact year-by-year sequence.

Additional empirical background: [Pfund et al. (2014), Training mentors of clinical and translational research scholars: a randomized controlled trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24667509/) tested a structured mentor-training curriculum. Its clinical/translational setting and mentoring-skill outcomes should not be presented as direct evidence of doctoral completion or publication gains in this lab. No effect-size or outcome claim was added to the website.

## AI-first research

The AI-first workflow is Daniel's account of recent practice, not a finding of the mentoring sources. The page expands his adversarial and game-theoretic description into competing explanations, skeptical perspectives, differing incentives, and weaknesses in evaluation. Idea refinement, rapid prototypes, and repeated changes in direction are retained.

Source checking, code inspection and testing, explanation of methods, evaluation against data, and a record of consequential changes express research responsibilities alongside that workflow. The page does not claim that AI agreement validates results or that this approach has demonstrated better mentoring outcomes. No particular vendor, subscription, automated review platform, or formal game-theoretic algorithm is asserted.
