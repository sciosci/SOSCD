Scientific Image Tampering Detection Based On Noise Inconsistencies: A Method And Datasets

Ziyue Xiang, Daniel E. Acuna

Preprint / working paper · arXiv:2001.07799 · January 21, 2020

DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2001.07799

Read the preprint Cite this workResources

Can image noise help identify manipulated scientific figures?

Scientific images have different properties from everyday photographs. This preprint develops a detector tailored to scientific imagery and tests whether inconsistencies in image noise can reveal manipulated regions.

What the study found

  • The method is trained and evaluated using manipulated western blot and microscopy images.
  • On the reported benchmarks, it outperformed the general-purpose image-tampering methods used for comparison.

How the study works

The work constructs scientific-image manipulation datasets and learns to detect noise inconsistencies associated with edits.

Scope and limitations

  • Benchmark performance on these image types does not establish performance on every scientific imaging modality.
  • A detector output is a signal to inspect the source material, not a determination of research misconduct.
